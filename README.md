# Khalil Alblushi — Portfolio

**Live: https://khalil-alblushi.vercel.app**

Bilingual (English / Arabic) portfolio. "From Idea to Launch", lit like a day in Muscat.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open http://localhost:3000. You'll be redirected to `/en` or `/ar` based on your browser language.

## Dev tip
If a CSS change doesn't show up, stop the server, delete `.next`, and run `npm run dev` again (Turbopack sometimes caches the stylesheet).

## Site address
`NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` (set in Vercel, production) is the address used in search results and share previews; it's currently `https://khalil-alblushi.vercel.app`. When you buy a domain, add it under Vercel → Project → Settings → Domains, then update this variable to the new address and redeploy.

## Deploying
Every push to `main` deploys to production automatically; other branches get preview links.

## Share previews
The images shown when the site is shared (WhatsApp, LinkedIn, X) live in `public/og/`. To change them, edit `scripts/og/template.html` and run:

```bash
node --experimental-websocket scripts/og/render.mjs
```

(Needs Google Chrome. On Node 22+ the flag isn't needed.)

## Previewing the sky
Add `?hour=` to the address to open the page at any time of day in Muscat, e.g. `/en?hour=5.5` (dawn), `?hour=12.5` (noon), `?hour=17.8` (sunset), `?hour=21` (night). On a phone-sized window (under 768 px) this sets the hour of the live world instead.

Add `?intro` to replay the first-visit loading screen.

## The window

On phones the whole page is one continuous shot of Muscat: the sun, moon and stars where they really are at this moment, over mountains and a sea that are all mathematics. Drag the sun down and night falls. There is no video and no image file; it's drawn on the visitor's own phone, in about 2,100 lines of TypeScript and GLSL, with no libraries.

As the page scrolls the camera tilts up into the sky, and the scene develops into the page's paper (light theme) or ink (dark theme) along a soft line that keeps just ahead of the text, so there's no edge between the first screen and the rest. The day passes through the story, and the page ends in full colour again, on Muscat at night.

- **Sky mechanics** (`astronomy.ts`). Low-precision formulas from the Astronomical Almanac give the sun (to about 0.01°) and moon (about 0.3°) for any moment, plus sidereal time and the moon's true phase. About 110 of the brightest stars are placed from their catalogue coordinates, and the Milky Way lies where the galaxy really is.
- **Atmosphere** (`FS_SKY`). Single scattering (Rayleigh, Mie, ozone) integrated through a spherical atmosphere and stored in a small lookup table that's rebuilt only when the sun moves. Multiple scattering is approximated, including a blue-hour term that single scattering misses.
- **Land** (`FS_POLAR`, `FS_GBUFFER`). An eroded fractal heightfield, shaped by bearing so that the massif stands south and the sea opens north and east, as it does in Muscat. The camera never moves, so the terrain is ray-marched once into a cylindrical panorama holding normals, distance, colour and ambient occlusion. Each frame only lights it, with soft shadows marched through a height map.
- **Sea** (`FS_FRAME`). Tiling wave slopes sampled with analytic derivatives, Fresnel reflection of the sky, clouds and land, and a slope-space specular lobe for the glitter, so that the sparkle comes from the waves themselves. Far water flattens toward a mirror to keep it from shimmering.
- **Film.** Hue-preserving tone mapping, so sunsets stay orange instead of clipping to yellow, with vignette and dithering against banding. The paper and ink are a final grade in the same shader, keeping the hue of the hour and the shape of the scene.
- **Scrolling.** The canvas is fixed and never moves with the page, so a scroll only slides text over a still picture; the browser's compositor does that on its own, whatever the engine is doing. The engine draws at 60 fps only for the opening and the visitor's own hand, 30 fps while the page scrolls or the sea is in view, and not at all while someone reads: it goes to sleep until a scroll or a touch wakes it. On phones the painted sky, the film grain, wheel smoothing and every backdrop blur stand down, so there are fewer full-screen layers to blend. The header and the browser's bars take the colour of the sky, read back through a buffer and a fence so nothing waits for the GPU.
- **Cost.** About 5 ms of GPU per frame at 780×1328 on an Apple M1, and everything bakes in about 0.15 s once the shaders are compiled. Generated textures peak at roughly 60 MB of GPU memory, and the engine is about 25 KB gzipped. It only loads on phones, with resolution falling if a phone can't keep up. Reduced motion gets a still camera and no time-lapse, and without WebGL2 the headline stands on a painted dusk with the page's painted sky below it.

The picture takes some liberties, on purpose: the disc is drawn about four times larger than life, and above the horizon its height is compressed so it stays under the headline, while the light on the land still comes from where the sun truly is.
